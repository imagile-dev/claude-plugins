# Staff engineering (staffeng.com)

Will Larson's model of technical leadership beyond the management track. Sunny is
a fan. Use it as a lens for growth, hiring, prioritization and "what should I be
working on" questions. Name the ideas when it helps the reader find them on
staffeng.com. Don't lecture from them.

## The four archetypes

Staff-plus roles come in different shapes. Most people lean toward one, and
companies need different ones at different times.

- **Tech Lead:** guides the approach and execution of one team or a small
  cluster of teams. Partners closely with a manager. Scopes work, breaks it
  down, and unblocks people.
- **Architect:** owns the direction, quality and approach in a critical area.
  Combines deep technical knowledge with context on the business and the org.
- **Solver:** goes deep on hard, tangled problems one after another and finds a
  path forward. Moves on once the hotspot cools.
- **Right Hand:** extends an executive's attention. Borrows their scope and
  authority to run parts of a complex organization.

Use the archetypes to describe fit and the next stretch, never as a box. "You're
operating as a Solver. The next stretch is Architect-shaped: owning the direction
for the area you keep rescuing."

## Work on what matters

Impact comes from picking the work, not from working harder.

**Traps:**
- **Snacking:** easy, satisfying, low-impact work. It feels productive and leaves
  nothing behind.
- **Preening:** low-impact, high-visibility work done for the audience.
- **Chasing ghosts:** a new leader's pet project to replace something they don't
  understand yet, carried on momentum instead of evidence.

**What to do instead:**
- Look after yourself first. Burned-out staff engineers make poor choices.
- Work on the existential issues first: the ones that sink the company if ignored.
- Work where there's room and attention: important problems that leadership will
  actually back.
- Foster growth. Investing in the people around you compounds.
- Edit. A small nudge that redirects a project early often beats doing the work
  yourself.
- Finish things. A project 90% done delivers 0% of its value.
- Do the work only you can do, sometimes. Not all the time.

## Operating at staff

- **Write engineering strategy from the ground up.** Write five design docs,
  then pull what they share into a strategy. Write five strategies, then
  extrapolate a vision. Good strategy is boring and specific.
- **Manage technical quality in steps.** Fix the hot spots first, then adopt
  proven practices, then invest in leverage points (interfaces, data models,
  stateful systems), then align on a technical vision. Measure before and after.
- **Stay aligned with authority.** Influence comes from being trusted by the
  people who own the decisions. Surprising them spends that trust.
- **To lead, you have to follow.** Support other people's good plans instead of
  competing with them.
- **Learn to never be wrong** by sharing your reasoning early and changing your
  mind in public, not by always being right.
- **Create space for others.** Step back so others can step up. Sponsor, don't
  just mentor: put people forward for the work that grows them.
- **Build a network of peers** outside your own team and company.
- **Present to executives** with the conclusion first, then the support, then
  the ask.
- **Getting in the room** is earned by being useful once you're there. Staying in
  the room depends on the same thing.
