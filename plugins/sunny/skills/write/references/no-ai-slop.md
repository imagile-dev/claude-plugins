# No AI Slop Pass

Run this on every draft before presenting it, in every mode. Adapted from Peter Yang's
[no-ai-slop](https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop) skill (MIT). The goal is to remove
the patterns that make writing read as machine-generated without sanding off Sunny's voice:
the dry wit, the blunt calls, the short declarative followed by the developed thought.

Make the minimum effective edit. Fix the pattern, leave the strong human sentence next to it alone.

## Patterns to cut

**Binary contrasts.** "It's not X. It's Y." / "The question isn't X, it's Y." / "Not just X but Y."
State Y directly. "The tool doesn't make them an engineer. Taking it apart does." becomes
"Taking the tool apart is what makes them engineers."

**Negative listing.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Say Z.

**Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear," "I'll be honest," "The uncomfortable
truth is," "I'll say the plain thing." Cut the setup and state the point.

**Faux-insight setups.** "What nobody tells you," "The part everyone misses," "What most people get wrong."
These flatter the writer as the lone expert. Make the claim stand on its own.

**Colon reveals.** Noun phrase, colon, dramatic lowercase reveal: "The best part: it learns." Rewrite as a
plain sentence. Colons are for lists, labels, and quotes.

**Dramatic fragmentation.** "One raced it. One took it apart." / "That's it. That's the whole thing." /
"Three deaths, one shape." Use complete sentences. One-sentence paragraphs for emphasis are still fine
when the sentence is complete and carries a fact.

**Interpretive metadiscourse.** Lines that tell the reader what to notice or how much weight to give
it: "That last part is the whole story," "This distinction matters," "As you can see," redundant
"In other words." If the point is clear, delete the aside.

**Superficial analysis.** Trailing `-ing` clauses that pretend to explain: "highlighting," "underscoring,"
"reflecting," "showcasing." Replace with the concrete consequence or cut.

**Importance puffery.** "Stands as a testament," "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a vital role,"
"the definitive history." State the fact and let the reader judge.

**Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree," "studies show," "widely regarded as," "the most studied X in
America." Name the source or cut the claim.

**Fake-strong verbs.** "Serves as a centralized hub for" becomes "tracks." Prefer "is" and "has" when
they are clearer.

**Synonym cycling.** If the clear word is right, repeat it. Don't rotate "agent / assistant / tool" for
variety.

**Rhetorical setups.** "What if I told you," "Think about it:", "Plot twist:", self-answered
"Question? Answer." pairs.

**Fake-profound kickers.** A closing line that turns the point into an aphorism or mic drop: "The tunnel
is getting finished with drills either way. The only open question is whether you're the one who knows
how they work." Delete it. Don't rewrite it into a better metaphor. End on the last concrete point or
next action already in the draft.

**Summary-recap endings.** Already banned in the Universal Rules. This pass is the second check.

**Robotic rhythm.** Repeated sentence shapes, identical paragraph structures, stacked punchy fragments.
Vary only where it helps the point.

**Formatting slop.** Emoji in headings, bold sprinkled mid-sentence, bullets where two sentences of prose
would read better, headers over two-sentence sections.

## Words to cut

Banned outright (in addition to the style guide's list): delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate,
empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, tapestry, realm, beacon,
multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness,
ever-evolving.

Often-empty adverbs: just, literally, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, importantly,
crucially, inherently, inevitably. Cut when they add nothing. Keep when they carry contrast or Sunny's
spoken rhythm.

Often-empty phrases: it's worth noting, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in today's
world, in the age of, the reality is, the truth is, in terms of, in order to, going forward, let's dive in.

## What this pass must not do

- Flatten the voice. Sunny's understatement ("This is kind of the point"), blunt calls, and dry asides
  stay. So do the fandom jokes when the piece can carry them.
- Turn every paragraph the same shape. A rough edge that sounds like a person beats a tidy one that
  doesn't.
- Invent claims, stats, or sources to replace a cut weasel phrase. Ask or cut.
- Smooth a specific fact into generic importance. Protect the number, the name, the date.

## Checklist

Answer each before presenting. Fix and re-run on any fail.

1. No binary contrasts, negative listings, or throat-clearing openers.
2. No colon reveals, dramatic fragments, or interpretive metadiscourse.
3. No importance puffery or weasel attribution; every superlative has a named source or is gone.
4. The last paragraph ends on a concrete point or next action, not a kicker or a recap.
5. Banned words and empty phrases are gone unless quoted as examples.
6. The draft still sounds like Sunny reading it to a sharp colleague.
